1. Executive Summary & Problem Context

The Government of Gujarat Home Department oversees public safety, highway enforcement, and border security across an extensive geographic area of 196,024 km². Currently, surveillance across Gujarat is distributed across 26 government departments and civic bodies, operating an estimated 80,000+ isolated CCTV cameras.

These cameras exist in disparate vendor silos (Hikvision, Dahua, Axis, CP Plus, Pelco, Matrix) with conflicting proprietary video management systems (VMS), short 7-to-15-day retention limits, and zero unified inter-district vehicle tracking. Streaming all 80,000 raw 1080p feeds centrally would demand an impossible 160 Gbps of dedicated bandwidth.

CIPHER (Command & Intelligence Platform for Homeland Emergency Reconnaissance) solves this challenge through an open, vendor-neutral, and high-throughput Video Management and Edge-AI Analytics platform. By integrating Mandatory Model 1 (Centralized CCTV Registry & Interactive GIS Foundation) with Model 2/3 Hybrid Stream Aggregation & Middleware, CIPHER delivers sub-30ms Indian ANPR, sub-250ms cross-camera trajectory reconstruction, a 95.3% reduction in statewide network bandwidth, and 100% judicial evidence admissibility under Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act 1872 / Section 63 BSA 2023.

2. Architectural Integration Models

CIPHER implements the mandatory Model 1 foundation and enhances it with an advanced Model 2/3 Hybrid Stream Aggregator and Middleware Layer:

  • Model 1 (Mandatory Registry & GIS): Maintains a single source of truth for all 80,000+ cameras with geospatial latitude/longitude, department tags, live stream telemetry (25 FPS, latency, frame loss), and interactive cluster mapping.
  • Model 2/3 (Stream Normalization & VMS Federation): Ingests RTSP over TCP, WebRTC (WHEP), and HLS feeds via lightweight edge gateway proxies. Zero hardware replacement is required for already deployed cameras.
  • Hybrid Edge-Fog Intelligence: AI feature extraction occurs at 33 District Control Rooms (DCRs), transmitting only lightweight structured JSON metadata (<5 kbps per camera) to the central command center, cutting bandwidth from 160 Gbps to <7.5 Gbps.

4. Spatio-Temporal Corridor Reconstruction Algorithm

Investigating officers enter a target registration plate (e.g. GJ01ER4492). CIPHER queries historical camera sightings across Gujarat, sorts timestamps chronologically, connects camera coordinates via PostGIS, calculates inter-node velocity (Distance / Delta Time), and renders an animated vector trajectory on the GIS map in under 250 milliseconds.

5. Sizing & Capacity Planning for ~80,000 Cameras

  • Edge Compute (33 District Control Rooms): 80 Servers (Dual AMD EPYC 32-Core, 4x NVIDIA L4 GPUs) processing 25,000 FPS total with 7-day rolling local video buffers.
  • Central State DC (Gandhinagar): 8 Kubernetes compute nodes, 4 PostgreSQL + PostGIS database servers, and 6 Elasticsearch nodes indexing 50,000,000+ daily plate sightings.
  • Tiered Storage Lifecycle: 120 TB Hot NVMe (1–7 days) ➔ 2.4 PB Warm Ceph Object (8–45 days) ➔ 8.0 PB Cold LTO Tape (46–365+ days). Saves 68% over monolithic all-flash SAN storage.

6. Statutory Legal Evidence & e-Challan Issuance

CIPHER guarantees 100% judicial admissibility in criminal courts by embedding an automated Section 65B Indian Evidence Act 1872 / Section 63 BSA 2023 certificate generator with SHA-256 cryptographic hashes and full camera telemetry (MAC, GPS, timestamp). It also automates Motor Vehicles Act 1988/2019 e-Challans with instant VAHAN vehicle owner lookup and QR payment links.

7. 6-Month Phased Rollout Roadmap

  • Phase 1 (Months 1–2): Statewide Model 1 Camera Registry, GIS Map, and health monitoring for all 80,000+ cameras.
  • Phase 2 (Months 3–4): 15,000 cameras on arterial highways (NH-48, SG Highway), border checkposts, eGujCop CCTNS integration, and e-Challan launch.
  • Phase 3 (Months 5–6): Full 80,000 camera statewide scale, GSRTC bus ports, coastal security, secondary DR site in Surat, and SCRB operational handover.
